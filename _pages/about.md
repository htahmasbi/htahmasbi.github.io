I am a **computational materials scientist** working at the intersection of **machine learning**, **high-throughput simulation**, and **materials discovery**.

My research focus: building, and just as importantly **evaluating**, machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) — deciding which models to trust, where they fail, and what the metrics actually mean. I combine AI/ML, density functional theory (DFT), and high-performance computing to accelerate materials discovery and connect predictions to physical reality.

- **Benchmarking & validation** — Led the benchmarking of leading universal MLIPs across elemental systems (MACE, MatterSim, PET), probing stability, transferability, and failure modes during global structure searches.
- **High-throughput MLIP development** — Trained neural-network interatomic potentials for aluminium, iron hydrides, and water using MongoDB/AiiDA-based automated workflows and custom PyTorch models.
- **From predictions to physics** — Validated large-scale neural-potential simulations against experimental IR spectra; discovered novel structures of TiO₂, ZnO, and near-stoichiometric MgO.

I completed my **Ph.D. in Condensed-Matter Physics** at the Institute for Advanced Studies in Basic Sciences (IASBS), with a visiting research stay at **Leiden University**, and am currently a postdoctoral researcher at [CASUS–HZDR](https://www.casus.science/) in Görlitz, Germany.
